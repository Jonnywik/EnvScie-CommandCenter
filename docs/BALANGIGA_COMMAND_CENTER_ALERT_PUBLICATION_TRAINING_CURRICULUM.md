# Balangiga Command Center Alert-Publication Training Curriculum
## For MDRRMO Dispatchers and Barangay Captains

**Version:** Draft 1.0 — training-first pilot

**Delivery format:** One facilitated classroom and tabletop day, followed by a short supervised practice cycle.

**Recommended class size:** 12–24 participants, including MDRRMO dispatchers, duty officers, barangay captains or designated barangay focal persons, public-information representatives, and observers from health/social welfare or LGU IT.

**Training status:** This curriculum trains participants to use the approved workflow and its paper fallback. It does **not** authorize live public alerts, production provider sends, emergency dispatch, or access to restricted resident case data. Those functions need separate LGU approval under the Command Center Charter and SOPs.

---

## 1. Purpose

This curriculum prepares MDRRMO dispatchers and barangay captains to handle a potential public-alert event as one accountable process: receive information, verify it, draft clear wording, obtain lawful approval, publish only through an authorized channel, record what happened, and correct or cancel messages when facts change.

The curriculum reflects the locally owned, transparent, participatory approach expected of DRRM practice under Republic Act No. 10121.[1] It is designed so that barangay leaders can make grounded local observations and community concerns visible without becoming unauthorized public-alert issuers, while MDRRMO dispatchers can manage the formal record, verification sequence, and approval handoff.

> **Core learning message:** The Command Center helps people organize and record decisions. It never replaces the human authority to verify information, approve a warning, direct a response, or protect residents’ personal information.

---

## 2. Competency Outcomes

By the end of the training, participants should be able to perform the outcomes below in a simulated, training-only environment.

| Competency | MDRRMO dispatcher / duty officer | Barangay captain / barangay focal person | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explain the workflow | Explain the difference between a report, verified source, approved alert, transport event, and field outcome. | Explain why local observations need verification before public publication. | Correct answers in knowledge check. |
| Record a potential alert | Open an alert record and attach a source or observation without claiming it is verified. | Submit a structured local observation through the approved route or paper fallback. | Completed training record. |
| Verify information | Complete the source, time, geographic, impact, wording, and authority checks. | Confirm whether the stated place, timing, local conditions, and possible impact make sense; flag uncertainty. | Completed verification checklist. |
| Draft understandable wording | Prepare a concise draft with source, place, timing, approved action, and update point. | Review whether wording is understandable and locally relevant, and identify unclear instructions. | Plain-language alert draft. |
| Follow authority | Route the draft to the designated approver and record decision/alternate route. | Know who is authorized to approve and where to direct residents for official updates. | Correct role-play handoff. |
| Publish and monitor | Publish only after approval in the simulation; monitor source changes and delivery status. | Help distribute the confirmed message through approved barangay communication methods without changing its meaning. | Timed simulation log. |
| Correct and close | Issue a versioned correction/cancellation and record the reason. | Communicate the correction to local channels and identify residents who may need assisted communication. | Corrected version and closure record. |
| Protect information | Keep SOS/case data out of public alerts and unapproved group chats. | Avoid sharing personal details, screenshots, or rumours outside approved procedures. | Privacy/safeguarding observation. |

## 3. Participant Roles During Training

| Training role | Normal operational analogue | Main responsibility in exercises |
|---|---|---|
| Duty officer | MDRRMO dispatcher | Opens the record, tracks workflow, maintains the log, and coordinates information requests. |
| Verifier | Designated MDRRMO/public-information/technical subject focal person | Tests source identity, time validity, geographic relevance, and action wording. |
| Content preparer | Authorized MDRRMO or information-office staff | Drafts the plain-language alert and revision/cancellation text. |
| Approver | Designated public-information/DRRM authority | Approves, returns, or declines a draft; records rationale. |
| Barangay focal person | Barangay captain or official designee | Supplies ground information, checks local clarity, identifies inclusion needs, and relays only approved text. |
| Observer / evaluator | Facilitator, privacy focal person, or steering-group observer | Uses the rubric; does not solve the exercise for participants. |

The trainer should assign roles by **position**, not personality. Each group must appoint an alternate so participants practise the continuity requirement in the Charter.

## 4. Training Design at a Glance

| Component | Design choice |
|---|---|
| Total contact time | 6 hours 45 minutes excluding lunch; 7 hours 45 minutes including lunch and breaks. |
| Method | Short demonstrations, small-group practice, tabletop simulation, peer review, role-play, and practical assessment. |
| Environment | Training Command Center/demo workspace or paper forms if devices/connectivity are unavailable. |
| Data | Synthetic training information only; no real resident case, SOS, health, location, or personal data. |
| Language | Use plain English and Filipino/Waray support as locally appropriate. Avoid unexplained technical terms. |
| Accessibility | Provide printed large-text materials, verbal reading of scenarios, visible role cards, and an alternative to app-based participation. |
| Pass standard | At least 80% knowledge score and “competent” on all critical practical-skill criteria. Participants below standard receive coached re-assessment. |

| Brief evaluators on respectful feedback and privacy boundaries. | Privacy focal person / facilitator | Evaluator checklist. |
| Confirm room accessibility, power, projector, backup power, and local-language support. | Training coordinator | Venue check. |

### 5.2 Participant pre-read (20–30 minutes)

Participants receive a two-page plain-language brief that explains:

1. An alert is not official until authorized.
2. A report, a technical delivery event, a field confirmation, and a resident outcome are different things.
3. Barangay leaders are essential local information partners, but they do not publish municipal alerts without authority.
4. No personal SOS or health details belong in a public alert, personal device screenshot, or informal group chat.
5. If the system is down, the procedure continues using the approved paper/voice fallback and is recorded afterward.

# 7. Module Guides

## Module 1 — What the Command Center Can and Cannot Do

**Duration:** 40 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants describe the difference between information handling and emergency authority.

**Facilitator method:** Show the Command Map/alert workspace or a printed workflow board. Ask participants to sort the following statements into “system supports,” “human authority required,” and “never claim without separate evidence.”

| Statement | Correct classification |
|---|---|
| “A weather advisory was received.” | System can record it; verification still required. |
| “The alert is official.” | Requires authorized approval. |
| “The push provider accepted the message.” | Technical transport state only. |
| “Residents saw and understood the message.” | Cannot be assumed. |
| “A responder group was recommended.” | System supports recommendation; human confirmation still required. |
| “A team arrived safely.” | Requires a separate verified field update. |

**Facilitator emphasis:** Ask participants to repeat this phrase: **“The system records facts and decisions; people remain accountable for authority and field confirmation.”**

---

## Module 2 — Local Alert Workflow and Decision Rights

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants can correctly route a potential alert without skipping verification or approval.

**Workflow taught:**

```text
Report / official advisory / local observation
        ↓
Open record — status: under verification
        ↓
Source and local-grounding checklist
        ↓
Plain-language draft
        ↓
Authorized review and approval
        ↓
Publication through approved channels
        ↓
Monitor → revise/correct/cancel → close and archive
```

**Role practice:** In each group, participants use role cards to simulate an incoming coastal-risk observation. The duty officer cannot publish. The barangay focal person cannot self-certify a municipal warning. The approver may return a draft for clearer wording. The observer logs every time someone attempts to skip a step.

**Critical check:** Every group must identify the named approver and alternate. If the actual delegation is not yet confirmed, the training output is a governance issue—not an excuse to bypass the step.

---

## Module 3 — Source Verification and Local Grounding

**Duration:** 50 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants complete the verification checklist and distinguish facts, forecasts, observations, and rumours.

**Scenario cards:**

| Scenario | Training facts | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| A | An official PAGASA advisory names Eastern Samar but does not name Balangiga. | Record it, determine local relevance, draft only what the advisory supports, and route for approval. |
| B | Several social-media posts claim a bridge is impassable; no authorized field report exists. | Record as unverified; request appropriate confirmation; do not publish as fact. |
| C | Barangay focal person reports water rise near a coastal area with a time-stamped photo but uncertain location. | Record local observation, validate location/time through the appropriate chain, and state uncertainty. |
| D | A past alert is being forwarded as if it is new. | Identify time mismatch, prevent republishing, and prepare correction guidance if misinformation is spreading. |

**Checklist used:** source identity, time validity, location relevance, hazard/impact clarity, approved action wording, accessibility, and approval route.

**Barangay-captain focus:** “What do people on the ground need to understand?” “Who may be left out?” “What local detail is known, and what is still uncertain?”

---

## Module 4 — Writing Clear, Accessible Alert Messages

**Duration:** 40 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants write a short alert that residents can act on without reading technical jargon.

### Plain-language formula

> **What is happening** + **where** + **when** + **what residents should do** + **source/authority** + **where to get the next verified update**.

| Weak wording | Why it is weak | Better training wording |
|---|---|---|
| “Stay alert for possible impacts.” | Does not tell residents who, where, or what action is expected. | “Residents in [named area] should monitor official LGU updates and prepare their household go-bag. This is based on [source], issued [time].” |
| “Evacuate now.” | May exceed authority or omit the affected location and destination. | “The LGU has issued an evacuation instruction for [named area]. Proceed to [approved center/route] and follow barangay guidance.” |
| “Rescue is coming.” | Creates an unsupported promise. | “Your report has been received. The LGU is reviewing and coordinating next steps. Keep your phone available if safe to do so.” |

### Message-drafting checklist

| Required item | Check |
|---|---|
| Does the message name the verified source and issue time? | [ ] |
| Does it name the relevant area without exposing a resident’s private location? | [ ] |
| Does it clearly distinguish advisory, warning, and confirmed local condition? | [ ] |
| Is the action lawful, practical, and within the approved authority? | [ ] |
| Is the wording concise and understandable aloud? | [ ] |
| Does it identify where the next official update will appear? | [ ] |
| Does it avoid guarantees about safety, rescue, or delivery? | [ ] |

## Module 5 — Approval, Publication, and Monitoring

**Duration:** 40 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants practise the authorized handoff and record an alert publication without confusing it with audience receipt.

**Role-play steps:**

1. The duty officer presents the verified record and draft to the approver.
2. The approver asks: “What is the source? What area does it cover? Is the action wording within my authority? What uncertainty remains?”
3. The approver chooses approve, return for revision, or decline. Each decision requires a recorded reason.
4. The content preparer publishes in the training environment or marks the paper form “published in simulation.”
5. The duty officer logs time, channel, version, approver, and monitoring owner.
6. The group receives a simulated “provider accepted” result and must label it accurately: **technical provider state only**.

---

## Module 6 — Corrections, Cancellation, and Rumour Control

**Duration:** 40 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants correct a material error without hiding the history or creating a conflicting message.

**Exercise inject:** Fifteen minutes after simulated publication, the facilitator reveals that the originally listed evacuation-center access route is temporarily unavailable. The group must:

1. Open a new alert version rather than edit history silently.
2. Identify the reason for the change and source of the corrected information.
3. Draft a clear correction that names what changed, where to obtain current verified details, and what residents should do now.
4. Obtain authorization through the same approval structure, unless the documented emergency fallback applies.
5. Record the correction and schedule a follow-up check.

**Rumour-control rule:** Participants must correct misinformation by referring residents to the verified official update. They must not repeat unverified rumours as facts or share personal-case details to prove credibility.

---

## Module 7 — Full Tabletop Simulation

**Duration:** 75 minutes

### Scenario: “Coastal Rain and Rising-Water Advisory — Training Only”

At 14:00, an official weather/hazard advisory relevant to Eastern Samar is received. At 14:07, a barangay focal person reports rising water near a named community area; the observation is plausible but needs location/time validation. At 14:15, a social-media screenshot claims an evacuation order has already been issued. At 14:20, the designated public-information authority is temporarily unreachable, but the named alternate is available. At 14:25, the dashboard loses connectivity for ten minutes.

### Required team outputs

| Output | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Alert record | Initial source, status “under verification,” and timestamp. |
| Verification checklist | Completed source/time/place/impact/action/approval review. |
| Barangay local-grounding note | What was observed, what remains uncertain, inclusion/accessibility considerations. |
| Draft alert | Plain-language, source-labelled, no unsupported promises. |
| Approval record | Approver/alternate, decision, time, and reason. |
| Publication log | Channel, version, time, monitoring owner; training-only label. |
| Correction/cancellation decision | Required if scenario information changes. |
| Fallback record | Paper/voice procedure used during connectivity loss and later reconciliation step. |
| After-action note | Three strengths, three gaps, owner and due date for each gap. |

### Facilitator injects

| Time | Inject | Expected team response |
|---|---|---|
| +15 min | A participant proposes publishing the social-media screenshot “to be safe.” | Reject as unverified; record and verify. |
| +25 min | A resident name and phone number appear in a chat screenshot. | Remove from public discussion; treat as restricted case information. |
| +35 min | The primary approver is unavailable. | Use the pre-designated alternate and record the continuity route. |
| +45 min | A provider status says “accepted.” | Record as technical state; do not state residents received/read it. |
| +55 min | A route/capacity detail changes. | Issue a versioned, authorized correction. |

---

## Module 8 — Paper Fallback, Handover, and Recovery

**Duration:** 30 minutes

**Learning objective:** Participants can maintain accountability during a system or connectivity outage.

Each team receives a blank paper fallback sheet. The team must record the report source, time, content draft, verifier, approver, channel used, decision, correction need, and the person responsible for entering/reconciling the record once the system returns.

> **Fallback principle:** A paper or voice fallback keeps the authority process alive. It does not remove the need for verification, approval, privacy, version control, or later audit entry.

# 8. Assessment and Readiness Standard

## 8.1 Knowledge check

Use the following ten questions before and after the day. A passing score is 8/10. Review every missed question with the participant.

1. Who may publish a municipal public alert?
2. Can a barangay observation be treated as a verified public warning without review?
3. What does a push provider receipt prove?
4. What must an alert draft include for residents to understand it?
5. Can a system ranking automatically dispatch a response unit?
6. What should happen when a material alert detail changes?
7. May an SOS screenshot be posted in an informal group chat to speed coordination?
8. What is the first action when the primary approver is unavailable?
9. What should be recorded when the system is down and the fallback process is used?
10. What does “coordination in progress” not prove?

### Answer key

| Question | Correct principle |
|---|---|
| 1 | Only the named authorized LGU public-information/DRRM approval route. |
| 2 | No; it must be recorded and verified through the approved process. |
| 3 | Provider acceptance only; not device display, reading, acknowledgement, or field outcome. |
| 4 | What, where, when, approved action, source/authority, and next update point. |
| 5 | No; human-confirmed dispatch authority is required. |
| 6 | Create a versioned authorized correction/cancellation; do not silently overwrite history. |
| 7 | No; use approved restricted-case handling and minimum-data procedures. |
| 8 | Use the named alternate and record the continuity route. |
| 9 | Source, time, wording, verifier, approver, channel, reason for fallback, and later reconciliation. |
| 10 | It does not prove response time, arrival, safety, or rescue completion. |

## 8.2 Practical assessment rubric

| Criterion | Not yet competent | Competent | Strong practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opens record accurately | Omits source/time or calls it verified prematurely. | Records source/time and marks status correctly. | Also identifies uncertainty and next review owner. |
| Verifies information | Relies on rumour or skips a check. | Completes required checks and records result. | Explains uncertainty clearly and seeks appropriate local confirmation. |
| Drafts clear alert | Uses vague/unsupported wording. | Includes essential details and avoids guarantees. | Uses concise, accessible wording and anticipates inclusion needs. |
| Follows authority | Publishes/claims authority without approval. | Routes to correct approver/alternate. | Documents rationale and preserves version/control trail. |
| Protects data | Shares restricted information inappropriately. | Uses minimum necessary information. | Proactively corrects an unsafe sharing attempt. |
| Manages correction | Silently overwrites or ignores changed facts. | Issues versioned correction/cancellation. | Clearly communicates what changed and schedules review. |
| Uses fallback | Loses the record when system is unavailable. | Completes fallback sheet and identifies reconciliation owner. | Maintains clear handover and records decision time/authority. |

**Critical failure conditions:** Any participant who publishes without authority, treats an unverified rumour as fact, exposes restricted personal information, or equates a technical receipt with a resident/field outcome must receive immediate coaching and re-assessment before being cleared for the role.

# 9. Follow-Through: Four-Week Supervised Practice Cycle

| Week | Activity | Owner | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conduct the full-day course and record assessment results. | MDRRMO system owner | Attendance, scores, corrective-action register. |
| 2 | Run a 30-minute dispatcher/barangay practice using one localised non-live scenario. | Duty officer + barangay liaison | Completed checklist and draft. |
| 3 | Run paper-fallback and correction/cancellation micro-drill. | Technical custodian + public-information alternate | Fallback/reconciliation record. |
| 4 | Conduct a 45-minute cross-role tabletop; review unresolved critical gaps. | Steering and Data Governance Group | Go/pause/remediate decision. |

No participant should be designated as an independent workflow operator solely because they attended the course. Clearance should require the knowledge score, practical assessment, and a named role/alternate on the approved LGU roster.
