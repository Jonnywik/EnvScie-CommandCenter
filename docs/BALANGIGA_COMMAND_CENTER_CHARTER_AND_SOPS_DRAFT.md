# Part I — Governance Charter

## Article 1. Establishment, Purpose, and Scope

### Section 1. Establishment

The Municipality of Balangiga establishes the **Balangiga Command Center** as an LGU-owned information, preparedness, and coordination capability under the lawful DRRM structure of the municipality. The Command Center shall support, but shall not replace, the authority and statutory responsibilities of the Mayor, MDRRMO/Municipal DRRMO, barangays, health and social-welfare services, PNP, BFP, and other competent agencies.

### Section 2. Purpose

The Command Center shall support the following lawful and approved purposes:

1. Maintaining verified operational context for hazards, alerts, evacuation centers, readiness resources, and training exercises.
2. Receiving, recording, routing, and monitoring emergency reports or SOS records within approved workflows.
3. Supporting source verification, authorization, publication, correction, and archival of LGU public-alert decisions.
4. Supporting human-led incident triage, dispatch recommendation, confirmation, safety monitoring, and handover.
5. Supporting inclusive community preparedness, partner coordination, after-action learning, and accountable records.

### Section 3. Operating boundary

The Command Center is an **information and coordination system**, not an autonomous decision-maker. It shall not independently:

| Prohibited autonomous action | Required control |
|---|---|
| Publish or cancel a public alert | Required source verification and authorized LGU approval. |
| Dispatch a response unit | Human confirmation by an authorized LGU role. |
| Treat a provider ticket or receipt as a resident acknowledgement or field outcome | Separate technical, operational, and field-status records. |
| Declare a disaster, road clearance, or center safety | Competent legal/operational authority and documented evidence. |
| Share identifiable case information with a partner | Lawful, minimum-data, approved referral/data-sharing process. |

### Section 4. Governing principles

The Command Center shall be guided by legality, public safety, human oversight, transparency, accountability, inclusion, privacy, proportionality, data minimization, operational continuity, non-partisanship, and community participation. Republic Act No. 10121 provides the national DRRM context for a comprehensive, participatory, transparent, and locally capable approach.[1]

### Section 3. Continuity and turnover

All authority shall be documented by **position, delegation, and alternate**, not solely by a person’s name. The system owner shall maintain an up-to-date handover package containing role contacts, pending decisions, access review status, active SOP version, current provider status, open incident actions, and next exercise date.

## Article 3. Information Classification, Privacy, and Audit

### Section 1. Information classification

| Class | Examples | Access rule |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved bulletins, public center information, verified public hazard summaries. | Public after authorization. |
| Internal operational | Duty roster, readiness status, non-public source notes, response resource capability. | Named LGU operational roles. |
| Restricted case | SOS location, health/safety details, household information, responder safety notes. | Minimum necessary authorized LGU users only. |
| Sensitive personal information | Health, age, political affiliation, and other legally sensitive categories. | Strictly necessary lawful processing only; heightened safeguards. |
| Partner aggregate | Non-identifiable readiness trends, approved resource requests, training outcomes. | Scoped partner access only. |

### Section 2. Privacy safeguards

The LGU shall apply transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality principles under the Data Privacy Act.[2] The following controls are mandatory:

1. Named accounts, least privilege, and no shared credentials.
2. Access and export logging for restricted records.
3. No case screenshots or personal data in unapproved chat groups.
4. Partner access defaults to aggregated data; identifiable referrals require a documented approved process.
5. Retention and deletion schedule approved by the privacy focal person and LGU legal review.
6. Incident response procedure for unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, or suspected misuse.

### Section 3. Audit record requirements

The Command Center shall retain an immutable or tamper-evident audit record, subject to lawful retention rules, for:

| Event | Required audit fields |
|---|---|
| Alert draft/publication/change/cancellation | Alert ID, version, source, verifier, approver, audience, time, reason, payload/channel. |
| Dispatch recommendation/confirmation/change | Incident ID, ranking inputs, reviewer, authority, unit, time, reason, status. |
| Case access/export/referral | User/partner, purpose, case reference, data category, authorization, time, outcome. |
| Provider event | Provider name, technical identifier, ticket/receipt/callback state, non-sensitive error code, time. |
| System configuration change | User, change, approval, environment, rollback plan, time. |

# Part II — Standard Operating Procedures

## SOP 1. Public Alert Publication, Revision, and Cancellation

### 1. Purpose

This SOP governs preparation, verification, approval, publication, revision, correction, and cancellation of LGU public alerts through approved Command Center channels.

### 2. Trigger conditions

This SOP may begin when an authorized source, verified local observation, formally received agency advisory, or approved incident assessment indicates a need to assess public information. A trigger is **not** itself a publication order.

### 3. Source-verification checklist

Before publication, the designated verifier shall document:

| Check | Required question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Source identity | Is the source an official agency, authorized LGU observation, or otherwise verified channel? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |
| Time validity | Is the information current, with a known issue/expiry time? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |
| Geographic relevance | Does it apply to Balangiga, a named barangay, coastal zone, center, or route? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |
| Hazard/impact clarity | Does it distinguish forecast, observation, advice, and confirmed impact? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |
| Action wording | Does it state only actions within LGU authority and avoid unsupported guarantees? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |
| Accessibility | Is the wording plain, concise, and usable through the approved channels? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |
| Approval needed | Is the appropriate public-information/DRRM approval route identified? | [ ] Pass [ ] Fail |

### 4. Alert levels and content rules

| Level | Use | Minimum content | Prohibited language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information | General preparedness or verified context. | Source, date/time, affected area, practical preparation, official update point. | “Emergency” without basis; claim that conditions are confirmed if they are forecast. |
| Advisory | Verified condition or credible risk requiring attention. | Source, area, timing, recommended approved action, next update time. | Unapproved evacuation order; guaranteed safety. |
| Warning | Authorized urgent public-risk message. | Clear hazard, precise affected area, authorized action, source, issue time, update/correction point. | Claims that responders are already present unless confirmed. |
| Cancellation/correction | Material change, error, expiry, or resolution of the public message. | Alert ID/version, what changed, current safe wording, source/authority, time. | Silent removal of a material message. |

### 5. Publication workflow

1. **Open an alert record.** The duty officer creates an alert record with initial source and status “under verification.”
2. **Verify.** The designated verifier completes the source-verification checklist and attaches/links the source evidence.
3. **Draft.** The content preparer drafts clear, factual language using the applicable alert level.
4. **Review.** The public-information authority checks authority, accuracy, audience, accessibility, and timing.
5. **Approve.** The authorized approver records approval electronically or by documented fallback method.
6. **Publish.** The technical/duty role publishes only through authorized channels in the approved payload/version.
7. **Monitor.** The duty officer monitors for source changes, public corrections needed, and technical delivery failures.
8. **Revise or cancel.** Material changes use a new version with a stated reason; they are never silently overwritten.
9. **Close.** The alert record notes expiry, cancellation, or supersession, with a final review.

### 6. Emergency fallback

If the Command Center is unavailable, the approved public-information authority may use the designated fallback communication method. The action must be recorded in the Command Center when restored, including source, wording, approver, channel, time, and reason for fallback.

### 7. Publication prohibitions

No alert may be published from a personal account, NGO account, vendor tool, or unapproved channel as an LGU public warning. Technical notification delivery must not bypass this approval workflow.

## SOP 2. SOS Intake, Triage, and Resident-Safe Status

### 1. Intake rule

The Command Center shall record a received SOS/report without implying verification, dispatch, or rescue. Duplicate suppression, technical receipt, and report validity are separate checks.

### 2. Status vocabulary

| Status | Meaning | Must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Submitted / handoff initiated | Device recorded an online submission attempt or SMS handoff. | LGU received it. |
| Received | Authorized API or verified gateway recorded the report. | Human review, dispatch, or responder arrival. |
| Acknowledged | Authorized LGU role reviewed/accepted the report in workflow. | A unit is en route or the person is safe. |
| Coordination in progress | Authorized human-confirmed work is occurring. | Guaranteed response time, arrival, or rescue completion. |
| Closed | LGU workflow closure reason has been recorded. | A clinical, safety, or personal outcome unless independently documented. |

### 3. Triage workflow

1. Review report completeness and technical duplicate status.
2. Identify the report’s current operational state and source reliability.
3. Verify information using appropriate, lawful channels; do not expose sensitive details to unapproved parties.
4. Record triage assessment, uncertainty, action owner, and next review time.
5. Update resident-safe status only through the approved workflow.
6. Escalate suspected immediate threats through the lawful DRRM/incident coordination path.

## SOP 3. Dispatch Recommendation, Confirmation, and Safety Monitoring

### 1. Purpose

This SOP ensures that dispatch tools support rather than replace human operational judgement.

### 2. Recommendation phase

The system may calculate or display candidate response groups based on availability, specialty, proximity, readiness, hazard context, and estimated route/ETA. This output shall be labelled **recommendation** and shall not create an assignment by itself.

### 3. Confirmation prerequisites

Before dispatch confirmation, the authorized dispatch authority or delegated operational role shall confirm and record:

| Check | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Incident reference | An active, identifiable incident/SOS record exists. |
| Authority | The confirmer holds a documented role/delegation for the decision. |
| Unit availability | Unit/group has confirmed available or appropriate status. |
| Capability fit | Required specialty/equipment is appropriate or exception is justified. |
| Safety context | Known hazard, weather, route, and communication risks have been considered. |
| Communication route | Unit contact method and fallback are available. |
| Decision rationale | Selection/override reason is recorded. |
| Resident wording | Any resident-facing status remains limited to safe, factual workflow status. |

### 4. Dispatch confirmation workflow

1. Operations lead reviews the recommendation and may choose any suitable qualified unit, including one not top-ranked, with a recorded reason.
2. Dispatch authority confirms or declines the proposed assignment.
3. The system records the decision, unit, time, confirmer, rationale, and planned contact method.
4. The operations lead notifies the unit through the approved operational channel; a notification transport event is not treated as unit acknowledgement.
5. The unit or authorized field channel confirms receipt/availability through the defined operational procedure.
6. The Command Center records subsequent verified states separately: contact confirmed, en route, on scene, unable to proceed, reassigned, returned, or closed.
7. Any route, hazard, or unit-safety change triggers reassessment; the system must not imply safety merely from a prior status.

### 5. Dispatch prohibitions

No dispatch may be confirmed solely because a system score is high, a partner requests it, a message was delivered, or an incident is politically sensitive. The system must allow a reasoned human override and must record it.

### 6. Unit-safety escalation

If a unit reports a hazard breach, loss of communication, unsafe route, insufficient equipment, or medical/safety concern, the operations lead shall immediately record the condition and escalate to the dispatch authority. The options are: suspend, reroute, reassign, request additional support, or continue only with recorded authorization.

## SOP 4. Partner and NGO Coordination

1. Partners participate only within a signed or approved scope, named focal point, and information tier.
2. The default partner view is aggregated readiness, resource offer, training, or approved referral information.
3. Partners may not publish LGU alerts, direct unit dispatch, or access raw SOS records without explicit lawful authorization.
4. Every partner resource offer must include availability, conditions, delivery contact, responsible owner, and expiry.
5. The LGU system owner reviews partner performance after exercises or deployments; access may be suspended immediately for privacy, safety, safeguarding, misinformation, or scope violations.

## SOP 5. Technical Notification and Gateway Events

### 1. Technical states

| Technical event | Operational interpretation |
|---|---|
| Push ticket accepted | Provider intermediary accepted a payload; not user/device receipt. |
| Push provider receipt `ok` | APNs/FCM accepted the payload; not device display, human reading, or acknowledgement. |
| `DeviceNotRegistered` | Disable/retire the token until secure re-registration; do not infer anything about resident safety. |
| SMS handset handoff | Resident device initiated a handoff; not gateway/LGU receipt. |
| Verified inbound SMS gateway callback | Gateway recorded a valid inbound event; may create/update the SMS-channel record under SOP 2. |
| Outbound SMS status callback | Provider transmission status; not human receipt or action. |

### 2. Release rule

No live APNs, FCM, Expo push, or SMS provider integration may be enabled until the provider-specific release gate is signed by the system owner, privacy focal person, technical custodian, public-information authority, and the applicable executive/dispatch authority.

---

## SOP 6. Corrective Action, Review, and Exercise

1. Every training exercise, alert correction, failed receipt, dispatch safety issue, privacy concern, or partner deviation shall create an auditable issue record.
2. The issue record shall contain severity, description, owner, due date, interim control, decision required, and closure evidence.
3. The steering group shall review high-severity issues before activating an affected function.
4. At least one tabletop exercise must test alert verification, publication approval, correction/cancellation, SMS handoff/verified receipt distinction, dispatch confirmation, unit-safety escalation, partner data boundaries, and paper fallback.
5. This charter and SOPs shall be reviewed after each major exercise, major system change, provider change, material privacy incident, or change in relevant law/policy.
