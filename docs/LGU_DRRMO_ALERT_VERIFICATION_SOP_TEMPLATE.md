## 1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes a repeatable process for the Local Government Unit Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (LGU DRRMO) to receive, assess, verify, approve, communicate, monitor, and close hazard alerts. It is designed for weather, flood, storm surge, landslide, earthquake, fire, public-safety, and similar incident alerts that may affect people, critical facilities, evacuation centers, roads, responders, or municipal operations.

The SOP is intended to make uncertainty visible. It requires staff to record what is known, the authority and freshness of each source, the affected location, the evidence that remains missing, the person who made each decision, and the deadline for the next review. It should be customized to local ordinances, delegated authorities, communication channels, hazard maps, mutual-aid agreements, and incident-command arrangements.

> **Core rule:** No evacuation instruction, road-closure notice, public danger message, or responder tasking should be approved from radar imagery, a static susceptibility map, a regional forecast, or a single unverified report alone. High-impact action requires fresh authoritative information, geographic relevance, recorded human verification, and authorized approval.

## 2. Scope and operating principles

This SOP applies to all alerts entered manually or received through the Command Center, PAGASA products, NDRRMC/OCD channels, Barangay DRRMC reports, responders, validated local instruments, partner agencies, and the public. It governs verification and decision-making; it does not replace legal mandates, emergency powers, incident-command doctrine, technical weather interpretation, professional rescue judgment, or the responsibility of the Local Chief Executive.

The following principles apply throughout the process.

| Principle | Required practice |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | Identify the issuing organization and source link or contact point for every live-condition claim. |
| **Freshness** | Record the observation or issuance time and the time received. Do not use expired evidence without explicitly renewing it. |
| **Locality** | Distinguish province-wide, municipal, barangay, watershed, road, and facility-specific information. |
| **Corroboration** | Seek independent evidence before high-impact escalation. |
| **Transparency** | Show the evidence, gaps, confidence, and reason for the current alert status. |
| **Human authority** | Automated scoring and maps support review; authorized personnel approve operational or public action. |
| **Do-no-harm** | Do not create a false all-clear, expose responders to unnecessary danger, or publish sensitive personal information. |
| **Auditability** | Preserve the assessment, decisions, evidence references, communications, acknowledgements, and closure rationale. |

## 3. Roles and decision authority

The LGU must fill the following role assignments before activating this SOP. One person may hold more than one role only when staffing is limited and the incident commander documents the reason.

| Role | Minimum responsibility | May not do alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Alert receiver / dispatcher** | Logs the alert, source, time, location, contact, and initial classification. | Approve high-impact public action. |
| **Verifier** | Checks source authority, freshness, geographic relevance, and field evidence. | Treat a static hazard layer as a live incident confirmation. |
| **Duty officer / assessment lead** | Assigns status, requests field verification, prepares the decision brief, and records uncertainty. | Issue an evacuation order unless locally authorized. |
| **Incident commander / DRRMO head** | Reviews operational readiness, approves tasking within delegated authority, and assigns alert owner. | Override a safety hold without documented rationale. |
| **Public information officer** | Releases only approved, plain-language public messages and monitors corrections. | Alter approved hazard facts or issue messages before authorization. |
| **Barangay focal person / field verifier** | Provides structured, time-stamped local observations and confirms receipt of requests. | Declare municipal-wide conditions based on a single local observation. |
| **Technical adviser** | Interprets specialized technical information within their qualification and advises the incident commander. | Substitute technical advice for authorized operational approval. |

## 4. Required evidence and evidence-quality checks

Every alert record must contain an evidence register. Staff must use the following fields, even when the answer is “unknown.”

| Evidence field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Alert ID | System-generated or manually assigned unique identifier. |
| Hazard type | Flood, heavy rainfall, storm surge, landslide, tropical cyclone, fire, earthquake, or other. |
| Source and authority | Issuing office, reporter name or agency, source URL/channel, and contact method where applicable. |
| Source timestamp | When the statement, observation, image, or report was produced. |
| Receipt timestamp | When the LGU received or entered it. |
| Geographic scope | Province, municipality, barangay, watershed, road segment, facility, coordinates, or uncertainty area. |
| Observed or forecast condition | Exact statement or measured observation; avoid rewriting a forecast as an observed event. |
| Affected people and assets | Known population, facilities, roads, bridges, evacuation centers, utilities, and responders exposed. |
| Corroboration | Independent sources that agree, disagree, or remain pending. |
| Data limitations | Stale data, unclear location, regional-only statement, unavailable communications, or incomplete report. |

### 4.1 Evidence categories

| Category | Typical examples | Use in verification |
|---|---|---|
| **Official current-condition evidence** | PAGASA warning, official flood advisory, validated government gauge, authorized agency bulletin. | May support a live-condition claim when fresh and locally relevant. |
| **Operational field evidence** | Time-stamped barangay report, responder observation, facility report, validated photo/video where safely obtained. | Confirms local impact, access, water level, damage, or urgent needs. |
| **Technical situational evidence** | RainViewer trend, satellite imagery, radar, route or sensor telemetry. | Supports monitoring and trend analysis; requires context and corroboration. |
| **Static exposure context** | Project NOAH flood-return-period, landslide-susceptibility, and storm-surge maps; local hazard maps. | Identifies places requiring attention; does not confirm that a hazard is occurring now. |
| **Public / unverified report** | Social-media post, SMS, phone call, or community report without verified origin. | Creates a verification task; does not by itself justify a public danger claim. |

## 5. Alert status and decision gates

Staff must assign one status at intake and update it whenever material evidence changes. The current status, owner, next review time, and reason must remain visible in the Command Center.

| Status | Minimum evidence threshold | Required action | Public communication allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monitor** | Broad forecast, radar trend, static exposure context, or unverified report. | Watch sources, identify exposed areas, set next review. | No location-specific danger claim. |
| **Verify** | One credible current source or a credible report with incomplete local confirmation. | Request field confirmation, assess readiness, preserve evidence. | Internal coordination only unless separately authorized. |
| **Review required** | Relevant evidence exists, but locality, freshness, corroboration, capacity, or safety remains uncertain. | Duty officer prepares a decision brief for authorized review. | Draft only; do not release. |
| **Approved action** | Fresh authoritative evidence, geographic match, appropriate corroboration, readiness check, and authorized approval are recorded. | Execute the approved operational action and monitor acknowledgements. | Yes, only within the approved message and authority. |
| **Closed / superseded** | Hazard resolved, alert expired, withdrawn, replaced, or no longer relevant. | Record the outcome, residual risks, and closure rationale. | Issue correction or all-clear only when authorized and supported. |

### 5.1 Minimum verification gate for high-impact action

Before approving an evacuation-related alert, road closure, high-risk responder tasking, or public danger message, the duty officer must document all applicable conditions below.

| Gate | Pass condition | If not met |
|---|---|---|
| Source authority | At least one recognized official or designated operational source is recorded. | Remain at **Verify** or **Review required**. |
| Freshness | Evidence is within the locally approved validity window or has been explicitly renewed. | Mark stale; request updated evidence. |
| Geographic relevance | The evidence names or credibly maps to the affected local area. | Treat as regional monitoring, not a local confirmation. |
| Corroboration | At least two independent evidence types support a high-impact conclusion, where practical. | Escalate field verification; document why single-source action is necessary if lifesaving urgency requires it. |
| Exposure | People, facilities, roads, or responders at risk are identified. | Clarify who or what is affected before public targeting. |
| Operational readiness | Responsible teams, communications, route/access, equipment, and safe alternatives are assessed. | Do not issue tasking that cannot be safely supported. |
| Authority | Required approving official is identified and decision is recorded. | Keep as draft or internal coordination. |

## 6. Source-freshness and geographic-relevance rules

The LGU shall adopt local validity windows in the table below and configure the Command Center to display when an input is near expiry or expired. The example intervals are editable and must be reviewed after exercises and incidents.

| Evidence type | Example validity window | Required interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Official warning or advisory | `[Use issuer validity; review at least every 30–60 minutes]` | Check for a newer issuance or cancellation. |
| Radar or satellite trend | `[15–30 minutes]` | Use for trend support; never as stand-alone proof of ground impact. |
| Local gauge / sensor | `[10–15 minutes]` | Confirm telemetry health, units, and location. |
| Field report | `[15–30 minutes, based on hazard]` | Record observer, exact location, condition, and ability to recontact. |
| Road or route status | `[15–30 minutes during active flooding]` | Reconfirm before movement; conditions can change rapidly. |
| Team location / check-in | `[15–30 minutes]` | Treat stale team location or check-in as a responder-safety concern. |
| Static hazard map | No live expiry; `[review source/version annually]` | Use only as exposure or planning context. |

## 7. Standard workflow

### Step 1 — Receive and log

The alert receiver opens a new record, assigns an alert ID, records the exact received message or source statement, captures timestamps, identifies the geographic scope, and names the initial owner. If the report indicates immediate threat to life, the receiver immediately informs the duty officer while continuing to log the available facts.

### Step 2 — Classify and screen

The verifier identifies the hazard category, source authority, freshness, locality, population or asset exposure, and whether the evidence is observed, forecast, modeled, or unverified. The verifier sets an initial status of **Monitor**, **Verify**, or **Review required** and states the next evidence needed.

### Step 3 — Request local confirmation

For a locally relevant hazard, the duty officer sends a structured verification request to the appropriate Barangay DRRMC focal person, responder, facility manager, utility operator, or partner agency. The request must ask for a time, exact place, observed condition, impact, passability, safety limitations, and callback method. Do not ask anyone to enter a dangerous location solely to obtain confirmation.

### Step 4 — Assess operational capability

Before tasking responders or issuing an action message, assess available response groups, personnel, equipment, communications, route status, evacuation-center capacity, medical needs, next check-in requirement, and responder-safety risk. If the Command Center displays an automated risk score, staff must read the contributing factors and holds; the score does not make the dispatch decision.

### Step 5 — Prepare the decision brief

The duty officer summarizes the evidence, confidence, affected area, likely consequences, recommended action, alternatives, information gaps, responder-safety issues, and draft public message. The brief must distinguish official current evidence from static hazard context and unverified reports.

### Step 6 — Approve, reject, or defer

The authorized official records one of the following decisions: **approve action**, **reject action**, **defer pending verification**, or **upgrade/downgrade monitoring**. The decision must state the authority, time, rationale, and next review deadline.

### Step 7 — Communicate and acknowledge

Only the approved message is released through approved channels. The communications officer logs the exact message, channels, time sent, target areas, acknowledgements, delivery failures, corrections, and related operational instructions. If the alert directs action, it must include the intended audience, place, timing, safe action, and verified source where appropriate.

### Step 8 — Monitor, update, and close

The alert owner monitors source refreshes, field reports, team check-ins, route access, and acknowledgements. Any material contradiction or expiry returns the alert to **Review required**. On closure, document the outcome, known impacts, unresolved issues, lessons, and whether an after-action review is required.

## 12. Escalation, suppression, and correction rules

The LGU shall use the following controls to avoid alert fatigue and misinformation.

| Situation | Required response |
|---|---|
| Evidence is stale, missing, or geographically unclear. | Keep or return the alert to **Review required**; request updated confirmation. |
| Sources conflict. | Document the conflict, lower confidence, seek independent confirmation, and avoid premature public claims. |
| Life-safety urgency exists but full verification is not feasible. | Take proportionate lifesaving action within authority, document the urgency and limits, and obtain follow-up confirmation immediately. |
| A public message contains an error or is superseded. | Issue a prompt correction through the same or more effective channels; preserve the original and correction in the audit trail. |
| Similar alerts recur. | Link records, avoid duplicate public messages, and state whether the new evidence materially changes the action. |
| An alert has no action or update by its review deadline. | Escalate to the duty officer and record the reason for delay. |

## 13. Performance measures and after-action review

The LGU DRRMO should review performance after major incidents, drills, and at least annually. The review should improve thresholds and staffing without retrospectively changing the original alert record.

| Metric | Definition | Suggested review question |
|---|---|---|
| Median verification time | Time from alert receipt to first evidence-based status. | Were staff, channels, and source access adequate? |
| Two-source confirmation rate | Share of high-impact alerts supported by two independent evidence types where feasible. | Were decisions adequately corroborated? |
| Geographic-match rate | Share of local actions with evidence that explicitly matched the target area. | Did the LGU overstate regional information? |
| Freshness compliance | Share of decisions supported by non-expired evidence. | Were review deadlines respected? |
| Acknowledgement time | Time for barangays, teams, and responsible officials to confirm receipt. | Which channels or areas need improvement? |
| False-alarm and missed-event findings | Outcomes identified through documented after-action review. | Do thresholds need adjustment? |
| Responder-safety holds | Number and reason for deferred or modified taskings. | Did the process prevent avoidable exposure? |
| Correction time | Time to correct a superseded or inaccurate public message. | Are communications controls effective? |

## 14. Training, exercise, and records management

The LGU DRRMO shall orient all duty officers, dispatchers, Barangay DRRMC focal persons, public information personnel, and response-group leaders on this SOP before typhoon season or equivalent local hazard season. Exercises should include incomplete information, conflicting reports, loss of communications, stale data, high-risk responder conditions, and the need to correct a public message.

All alert records, evidence references, approvals, messages, acknowledgements, and after-action findings should be retained according to the LGU records-retention policy and protected according to applicable privacy, security, and public-information rules. Access to personally identifiable incident information should be limited to personnel who need it for their role.
