## Slide 1 — Balangiga needs a clear operational picture

The municipality sits on the southern coast of Samar facing Leyte Gulf, with the Balangiga River meeting the coast. That geography creates a practical need to connect weather intelligence, flood and storm-surge awareness, evacuation readiness, and field response in one place.

PAGASA reports an average of 20 tropical cyclones entering the Philippine Area of Responsibility each year, with about 8 or 9 crossing the Philippines. The official Eastern Samar profile lists Balangiga as a 13-barangay municipality with 14,085 residents in the 2015 census.

Source footer: PAGASA; Province of Eastern Samar.

## Slide 2 — The response gap is operational, not only technical

During a typhoon, flood, or storm-surge event, the challenge is not simply receiving more data. The challenge is deciding what is verified, who needs help, which evacuation option is still usable, and which response unit can safely act.

Four recurring gaps frame the solution: delayed or inconsistent alerts; limited visibility into evacuation-center capacity; SOS requests that compete for attention; and fragile communication when power or mobile signal is unstable.

Source footer: Code for Resilience project brief and operational requirements.

## Slide 3 — The website turns scattered signals into one operational picture

Code for Resilience is a browser-based LGU command center that brings verified alerts, SOS triage, GIS layers, evacuation capacity, response groups, communications, notifications, and audit records into a single operating surface.

The system is designed for the moment when the LGU must move from “What is happening?” to “What is the safest action we can confirm now?”

## Slide 4 — Four feature pillars support the full response cycle

**See the situation.** Verified alerts, operational metrics, live GIS layers, hazards, resources, SOS locations, and evacuation centers.

**Prioritize life safety.** A triage queue, location confidence, severity filters, dispatch guardrails, and an auditable SOS workflow.

**Move the right resources.** Response-group roster, availability, specialties, position freshness, hazard-aware routing, and assignment controls.

**Keep coordination resilient.** Communication log, audio-dispatch simulation, SMS and in-app notification receipts, acknowledgement states, retries, and durable audit events.

## Slide 5 — From signal to action in five controlled steps

**1. Detect.** Receive official alerts, field reports, SOS requests, and resource updates.

**2. Verify.** Confirm the source, severity, location confidence, and operational relevance.

**3. Decide.** Compare hazards, evacuation capacity, response-group readiness, specialty match, distance, and route safety.

**4. Dispatch.** Assign a unit, send the instruction through available channels, and record the action.

**5. Confirm.** Track delivery, acknowledgement, arrival or handover, and the next operational decision.

## Slide 6 — What the LGU can see at a glance

The Overview surface combines four high-value views: situation metrics for untriaged SOS, critical alerts, open centers, and residents at risk; a live GIS map with hazard, resource, SOS, center, and route layers; a verified-alert feed; and the priority SOS queue paired with evacuation capacity.

The remaining tabs deepen the picture without changing the operating model: Live SOS, Verified Alerts, Risk Map, Evacuation Centers, Resources, Response Groups, and Communications.

## Slide 7 — Potential benefits for Balangiga operations

**Faster decisions:** one shared picture reduces the time spent reconciling separate lists, feeds, and messages.

**Safer movement:** hazard overlays, evacuation-center visibility, and route guardrails help operators avoid unsafe segments.

**More reliable coordination:** assignment notifications expose delivery and acknowledgement state instead of assuming a message was received.

**Stronger accountability:** dispatches, reviews, handovers, and operational changes are recorded as auditable events.

**Continuity under disruption:** the broader platform is designed around offline-first SOS behavior, cached operational records, and redundant communication paths.

These are operational benefits the system is designed to enable; actual impact depends on data quality, local procedures, connectivity, and adoption.

## Slide 8 — Built for Balangiga. Designed to scale.

Code for Resilience gives the LGU a practical digital layer for preparedness, response, and recovery—grounded in local geography, visible to the command center, and accountable at every action.
